#  Unsolved Headstone Mysteries

Since 2006, Friends of Toowong Cemetery and [The University of Queensland](https://social-science.uq.edu.au/undergraduate/archaeology) have uncovered headstone fragments during [archaeological digs](archaeological-digs/).

More than 150 lost headstones have been reunited with the people they commemorate. Yet a handful of fragments still refuse to give up their secrets. A few letters. Part of a date, A fragment of a name.

Somewhere in historical records, newspaper notices and cemetery archives are the clues needed to identify them.

Explore the mysteries below and see if you can help solve one.

## How to investigate

Be a digital detective:

- These headstone fragments most likely come from Paddington Cemetery (aka North Brisbane Burial Grounds).
- They were found with other headstone fragments we've already identified (either [headstones dumped in Toowong Cemetery  by the Ithaca Council](lost-paddington-headstones/), or [headstones moved to Toowong Cemetery  when Paddington Cemetery closed](moved-paddington-headstones/).
- [A list of headstones moved or set aside](https://brisbane.recollectcms.com/nodes/view/38396) (and later dumped) was published by the Queensland government.
- Use the [Queensland Government's Family History Research Service](https://www.familyhistory.bdm.qld.gov.au) to find Death records.
- Use [Trove](https://trove.nla.gov.au) to find Family Notices in historic newspapers. Sometimes notices are in newspapers from other States.
- The headstones may also come from Toowong Cemetery  where [over 1000 were demolished in the 1970's](demolished-toowong-headstones/) by the Brisbane City Council.

Visit Toowong Cemetery:

- Visit the [dig site](https://maps.app.goo.gl/3UWN6RSxXK4nwRSQA) and perhaps find another fragment of the headstone to piece together the answer. 


## What Happens If You Solve One?

Your discovery will help restore a forgotten piece of Queensland history.

- We'll publish the person's story and the evidence behind the identification.
- If you wish, we'll acknowledge your contribution on the webpage and in social media.
- If you used a new research technique, we'll share it  so other volunteers can learn from your approach.
