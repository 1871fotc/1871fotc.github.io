- [MAGISTERIAL INQUIRY, (1869, February 8). The Brisbane Courier (Qld. : 1864 - 1933), p. 2.](http://nla.gov.au/nla.news-article1308396)

> MAGISTERIAL INQUIRY, <br>
> A MAGISTERIAL inquiry was held by Massie on Friday, touching the death of John Lewis Ashworth. The following evidence was taken :- <br>
> John Ahern of the Sportsman's Arms, deposed I have known the deceased the last fifteen months ; he was a commission agent at Maryborough ; he came to Brisbane last Tuesday, and stopped at our house ; he was between 30 and 35 years of age ; he was short and stout ; he did not complain of being unwell when he first came to the house ; since I knew deceased, he was not in the habit of drinking much ; he only drank in moderation ; he told me his object in coming to town was to get some money that had been sent him from home through Messrs. Little and Browne ; he was in and out during the day of his death (Thursday last) ; last saw him alive about 11 o'clock in the' morning of that day ; he was perfectly sober ; to the best of my belief he had not had more than two or three nobblers that day ; about half-past 7 o'clock in the evening, two men came to enquire for him ; I had not seen him myself since the time I mentioned in the morning ; I went to see if he was in his bedroom ; the knob of the door was loose, and I could not open it; I then went to the window which was open, and called deceased by name ; he made no reply , went into the room through the window, and found deceased lying on his left side on the outside of his bed ; I thought he was asleep, and shook him ; I found was as hot as fire, and wet from perspiration ; I returned at once to the man who had been inquiring for his, named Shapcott, and told him I could not wake the deceased ; I did not then think he was dead, he was so hot and limber ; Dr O'Doherty was sent for, came within a quarter of an hour from the time I first saw the deceased and pronounced him dead ; the room where de ceased was found was about ten feet square ; there was only one window to it.  <br>
> Patrick Hanlow, bootmaker, deposed, I lodge at the Sportsman's Arms ; dined there on Thursday last, deceased was also at the table, noticed he ate very little ; he took no grog or liquor ; he complained very much of heat ; last saw him alive about half-past 1 o'clock - he was then in the parlor, after dinner, lying on the sofa. <br>
> William Edwin Shapcott, gold miner deposed : I have known the deceased four years he used to be clerk to Skyring, the butcher ; he was lately commission agent at Maryborough called at Ahern's on Thursday to see him ; first called at 4 o'clock in the afternoon ; was then informed he was out ; returned in an hours time, and was again informed he was not in; Ahern called again shortly after 7 o'clock ; went to look for him ; on his return, he said he had shaken deceased, but could get no answer ; we returned to the room, and found deceased lying , dressed, all but his coat, on his bed, his shirt was unbuttoned at the throat, and open in front ; saw that he was dead ; deceased had told me he was a single man.  <br>
> George Gilham, a member of Burton's company, deposed : I came to Brisbane in the Gympie coach last Tuesday ; deceased was also a passenger ; he complained of the heat but not more than I did myself; last saw him alive at Willett's on Thursday afternoon about 3 o'clock ; he had a glass of ale only ; he said he was going home, and should have read ; about four or five days before we left Gympie, I heard deceased had had a fit. <br>
> Christopher Blunden, Acting Sergeant of police, deposed : I received information of the death of the deceased about 8 o'clock Thursday evening ; went to the room where he was found, searched his pockets and found in them £41 9d. in cash ; there was a valise in his room containing amongst other things, a pocketbook in which was a cheque for £35, drawn George Skyring ; a gold ring was afterward given me by Constable Burke, which he had taken from deceased's finger, when they were placing him in the coffin ; made an exact inventory of all the property pointed out to me belonging to deceased, and took charge of it. <br>
> Kevin Izod O'Doherty, a duly qualified medical practitioner, deposed : I made a post mortum examination of deceased this day (Friday) was called in to see him about 8 o'clock on the day evening ; found him lying dead on top of the bed, in a small bedroom ; head and face were extremely congested; there was a frothing at the mouth and an intense heat over the whole body ; on stripping the body on Friday, found the head and neck black, and the general surface of the body congested ; there was no evidence of any external injury ; on opening the head and removing skull cap found the surface of the brain much congested, blood and serum were fused on the surface of the brain ; a good of serum was also found in the entire brain ; on opening the chest found the organs healthy ; the cause of death was congestion of the brain, most probably caused by the excessive heat of the weather ; deceased was …dently of an apoplectic habit. <br>
> The finding was in accordance with the …cal evidence,  <br>
> An order was made that all the property of the deceased should be handed over to George Edward O'Brien, acting for the Curator of Intestate Estates.
