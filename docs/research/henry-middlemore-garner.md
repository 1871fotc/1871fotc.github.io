> CORONER'S INQUEST.
>
> An inquest was held yesterday morning, at Mr. Smith's boarding-house, Mary-street, on the body of a young man, named Henry Garner, who died suddenly on the previous day. 
>
> John Cadbury deposed that he had been lodging with deceased, in the same house, for the past two months; deceased was somewhat given to intemperance ; lately he had been out of employment; on the previous day, witness was informed by Miss Smith that she thought deceased was in a fit; witness found him quite insensible, and bathed his forehead with cold water; a doctor was sent for, and Mr. Dent, Dr. Hancock's assistant, was quickly in attendance.
>
> Howard Smith deposed that he had known deceased for three or four years, during the whole of which time he had been addicted to intemperance; he had abstained from drink for some months, but then returned to his old habits; he had been in the colony about four years, and was twenty-four years old ; witness had known deceased to take laudanum and morphia when he could not get drink; about 2 o'clock on the previous after-noon, a young man who slept in the same room as deceased told witness that Garner was lying on his bed insensible; witness went into the room and saw deceased ; a two-ounce bottle of laudanum, three-parts full, was on the table; witness thought he was in a stupor, and did not disturb him, having seen him like that before ; about an hour and a quarter afterwards witness was again called to see deceased, and went to his room at the same time as a surgeon who had been previously sent for ; deceased was breathing hard, and appeared to be somewhat convulsed; witness had seen him in a similar fit before; witness was almost certain that he had nothing to drink on that day, and had no reason to believe that he would take anything to destroy himself purposely.
>
> James Scott Elliott deposed that he had slept in the same room as deceased for a month ; the latter always took drink when he got a chance ; witness never saw him helplessly drunk ; at 2 o'clock in the afternoon of the previous day witness saw deceased lying on the bed, and the bottle of laudanum upon the table, he informed Miss Smith of the circum-stance ; he saw no more of deceased.
>
> James Tomlin deposed, that he saw deceased on the previous day, at the Post-offce ; he said he was going home in the Charlie Palmer, and offered to take any message for witness; he was fond of a glass if he could get it.
>
> William Hamersley Spode deposed that he was cousin to deceased ; the latter would oc-casionally drink to excess and then leave off; he had a fit in witness' house eighteen months ago ; he was then a teetotaler ¡ the fit lasted for a quarter of an hour or twenty minutes ¡ his brother died of a somewhat similar fit at Cal-cutta ; deceased was a very quiet man.
>
> Benjamin Smith deposed that he was an as-sistant to Mr. Costis, the chemist, and knew deceased ; on the previous day he supplied the latter with a two ounce bottle of laudanum; he said it was for his own use ; he had been drink-ing hard lately. and could not rest at night without taking laudanum ; he had been a con-stant customer for laudanum and morphia, and said that he could take half an ounce of the former without injuring himself; he appeared quite sober at the time he got the laudanum, and said he was going home in the Charlie Palmer ; Mr. Howard Smith told witness, about 1 o'clock, that deceased had taken laudanum and was sleeping very quietly ; witness said he did not apprehend any evil consequences, but ad-vised that a medical man should be sent for if the deceased got worse.
>
> Thomas Dent stated that he was medical assistant to Dr. Hancock, and had been called upon to see deceased upon the previous day ; when he arrived he saw that deceased was too far gone, and shortly afterwards he died; witness believed that death had been caused by an overdose of laudanum.
>
> Verdict : That deceased had been accidentally killed by taking an overdose of laudanum, but that he had no intention of committing suicide.
