# Amos Braysher

**ca. 1836 — 27 September 1871**

## Amos Braysher <small>([29A](https://maps.app.goo.gl/tgb8biUeEF9NnF499))</small>

### Headstone

<!-- move to /dig-finds -->

![Amos Braysher's headstone](../../assets/dig-finds/amos-braysher-headstone-2012.jpg){ width="40%" class="full-width" loading=lazy }

<small>*A fragment of Amos Braysher's headstone discovered in a Toowong Cemetery Archaeological Dig in 2012.*</small>

- Erected: North Brisbane Burial Grounds, Episcopalian section. After 27 September 1871
- Moved to: North Brisbane Burial Grounds, Reserved Area. ca. 1914. Recorded as Amos Braysher
- Demolished and disposed of in: Toowong Cemetery, Portion 29A. ca. 1930
- Discovered: Toowong Cemetery, Portion 29A near Section 72. ca. May 2012

### Inscription

> Sacred <br>
> to the memory of <br>
> Amos Braysher  <br>

- [Advertising (1861, July 10). The Queensland Guardian (Brisbane, Qld. : 1860 - 1863), p. 1.](https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/290950008?searchTerm=amos%20braysher#)

> METROPOLITAN FAMILY HOTEL, Opposite A. S. N. Co.'s Wharf, Mary and Edward Streets, Brisbane. MR. JAMES MOONEY begs to inform his Friends and the Public, that he has OPENED THE NEW HOTEL Recently erected by him at the Corner of Mary and Edward Streets, Where Superior Accommodation is provided. The Hotel Arrangements are under the charge of Mr. AMOS BRAYSHER, late Steward of the Steamer Telegraph. Families and Gentlemen requiring the comfort of a home will find every requisite in this Establishment. Wines & Spirits of the Choicest Quality GOOD STABLING. N.B.— Passengers per Sydney and Ipswich Steamers will find this a most healthy and convenient House. Can be called at any hour.

- [BYGONE BRISBANE. (1908, January 19). Truth (Brisbane, Qld. : 1900 - 1954), p. 6.](https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/206489047?searchTerm=Braysher#)

> BYGONE BRISBANE <br>
> The Paddington Cemetery. <br>
> Amos Braysher, who died on September 27, 1871, aged 35, was the landlord of "Braysher’s Hotel," now the Metropolitan, Edward-street. His widow married Duncan, and after Duncan died, Mrs. Duncan KEPT THE HOTEL FOR YEARS. Mrs. Duncan's Metropolitan Hotel was the favorite hotel for squatters in those days and probably then the best hotel in Brisbane.
